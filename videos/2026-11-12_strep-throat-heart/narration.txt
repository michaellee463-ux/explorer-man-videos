How can a simple sore throat... end up damaging your heart? Meet group A strep. It's a bacterium, not a virus. It spreads through close contact, then latches onto the cells lining your throat, and your tonsils. There, it releases toxins that punch holes in your cells. Your immune system rushes in. And that battle brings the swelling, the fever, and the burning pain. But in some people, if strep goes untreated, the antibodies made to fight it... attack the heart instead. That's rheumatic fever. It can scar heart valves for life. And rheumatic heart disease still kills hundreds of thousands of people, every year. To prevent it? A quick throat swab, and antibiotics.
