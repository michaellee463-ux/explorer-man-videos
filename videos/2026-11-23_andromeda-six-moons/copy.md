## YouTube title
There's a Galaxy in Your Sky 6× Wider Than the Moon… So Why Can't You See It? 🌌🤯

## YouTube description
The Andromeda Galaxy is about six times wider than the full Moon in our sky. It's our closest big neighbor galaxy: around a trillion stars, about 2.5 million light-years away. The light reaching you tonight left it about 2.5 million years ago, long before humans like us existed. It's heading toward us at about 250,000 mph (roughly 70 miles closer every second). For years scientists said it would crash into the Milky Way in a few billion years, but a 2025 study using the Hubble and Gaia telescopes found it's about a 50–50 chance in the next 10 billion years. Even if they merge, stars are so far apart they'd almost never hit each other. So why can't you see it? Most of it is too faint for your eyes: from a dark sky you only see its bright center, a faint smudge about as wide as the Moon. A long-exposure photo reveals all six Moons' worth. It's one of the farthest things you can see with your own eyes.

Sources: NASA / ESA Hubble, STScI, ESA/Hubble 2025 (Sawala et al.), UC Santa Cruz, Astronomy.com, Las Cumbres Observatory, Smithsonian.
