## Sources
- [WIKI-KOLA] Wikipedia, Kola Superdeep Borehole — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kola_Superdeep_Borehole — "attaining a maximum true vertical depth of 12,262 metres (40,230 ft) in 1989"; "Drilling at Kola SG-3 began on 24 May 1970"; "has been the deepest human-made hole on Earth since 1979"; "Drilling penetrated about a third of the way through the Baltic Shield of the continental crust"
- [WA-KOLA] WorldAtlas, The Kola Superdeep Borehole — https://www.worldatlas.com/articles/the-kola-superdeep-borehole.html — "This particular hole reached a depth of 12,262 meters."; drilling started "on May 24th, 1970"; "It took nineteen years for the scientists to drill the deepest hole"; "The SG-3 Borehole is considered to be the world's deepest borehole."
- [USGS] USGS, Inside the Earth — https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/dynamic/inside.html — "about 12,750 kilometers (km) in diameter"; oceanic crust "generally extending only to about 5 km"; continental crust "averages about 30 km"; mantle "approximately 2,900 km thick"; "a 2,200 km-thick liquid outer core"; "a 1,250 km-thick solid inner core"; "As the Earth rotates, the liquid outer core spins, creating the Earth's magnetic field."
- [BRIT] Britannica, Plate tectonics — Earth's layers — https://www.britannica.com/science/plate-tectonics/Earths-layers — continental crust "typically 40 km (25 miles) thick", oceanic "about 6 km"; mantle "continues to a depth of 2,900 km (1,800 miles)"; "contains 83 percent of Earth's volume"; core "extends to the centre of Earth, some 6,370 km (nearly 4,000 miles) below the surface"; "At a depth of about 5,100 km (3,200 miles), the outer core transitions to the inner core"; "a liquid rich in iron and nickel"; "the inner core is solid because of the tremendous pressures that exist near Earth's centre"
- [SPACE] Space.com, Earth's layers — https://www.space.com/17777-what-is-earth-made-of.html — mantle "making up 84% of the planet's total volume"; outer core "approximately 1,367 miles (2,200 km) thick", "composed of liquid iron and nickel"; "The swirling motion of the outer core generates Earth's magnetic field"; inner core "radius of about 759 miles (1,221 kilometers) and a pressure of about 3.6 million atmospheres (atm)"; "about as hot as the surface of the sun (about 9,392 degrees F or 5,200 degrees C)"; "the immense pressure from the rest of the planet keeps the inner core solid"
- [LS-CORE] Live Science, Earth's Core 1,000 Degrees Hotter Than Expected (2013) — https://www.livescience.com/29054-earth-core-hotter.html — "now estimated at 6,000 C (about 10,800 F)"; "That's as hot as the surface of the sun."; "at a pressure of 3.3 million atmospheres (or 3.3 million times the atmospheric pressure at sea level)" (inner/outer core boundary); "The Earth has a solid inner core surrounded by a liquid outer core"
- [LS-FALL] Live Science, How Long Would It Take to Fall Through the Earth? (2015) — https://www.livescience.com/50312-how-long-to-fall-through-earth.html — old figure "was about 42 minutes and 12 seconds"; "Klotz finds the fall would take only about 38 minutes and 11 seconds"
- [KLOTZ] A. R. Klotz, The Gravity Tunnel in a Non-Uniform Earth, arXiv:1308.1342 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.1342 — "Assuming a uniformly dense Earth, it would take 42 minutes"; "The time taken to fall along the diameter is found to be 38 rather than 42 minutes."
