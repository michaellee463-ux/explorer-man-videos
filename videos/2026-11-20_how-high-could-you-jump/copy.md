## YouTube description
Same jump, seven worlds. Take the push-off of a solid 0.5 m (20 in) standing jump on Earth and try it across the solar system:

Earth: 0.5 m
Mars (38% of Earth's gravity): 1.3 m (4.3 ft), 1.7 seconds off the ground
The Moon (17%): 3.0 m (10 ft), about the height of a basketball rim, 3.9 seconds
Pluto (6%): 7.9 m (26 ft), higher than a giraffe, 10 seconds
Enceladus, a moon of Saturn (1.2%): 43 m (142 ft), about a 14-story building, 55 seconds
Phobos, a moon of Mars (0.06%): about 900 m (3,000 ft), higher than the Burj Khalifa, about 20 minutes off the ground
Comet 67P: your jump (3.1 m/s, 7 mph) is about three times the speed needed to escape, so you never come down. You end up orbiting the Sun.

How it's worked out: the same take-off speed everywhere (3.1 m/s), no air resistance, suit weight not counted. Phobos uses its average gravity. Its gravity is uneven, and in its weakest spots this same jump could escape Phobos.

Which world would you jump on? One word. 👇
