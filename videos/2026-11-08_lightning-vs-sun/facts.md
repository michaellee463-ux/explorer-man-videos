# Fact sheet — "Lightning vs. the Sun" (Short, 1080×1920)

Only figures in this sheet may appear in the video, thumbnail, title or description. Derived figures come from `compute.py` (output in `compute_output.txt`).

## Sources
- [NASA-T] NASA Science, Temperatures Across Our Solar System — https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/temperatures-across-our-solar-system/ — "The part of the Sun we call its surface – the photosphere – is a relatively cool 10,000° F (5,500°C)."
- [BR-SUN] Britannica, 6 Questions About the Sun Answered — https://www.britannica.com/list/6-questions-about-the-sun-answered — "The surface of the Sun (or its outer visible layer, called the photosphere) is about 10,000 degrees Fahrenheit (5,537 degrees Celsius)"
- [ESA] ESA, Anatomy of the Sun — https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2020/01/Anatomy_of_the_Sun — photosphere "between 4500 and 6000 °C"
- [BR-CG] Britannica, Thunderstorm: Cloud-to-ground lightning — https://www.britannica.com/science/thunderstorm/Cloud-to-ground-lightning — "Peak temperatures in the channel are on the order of 30,000 °C (50,000 °F)", "about five times hotter than the surface of the Sun"
- [BR-TH] Britannica, Thunderstorm: Thunder — https://britannica.com/science/thunderstorm/Thunder — plasma "at least 30,000 °C (50,000 °F)"; channel "expands at a supersonic rate"; "resultant shock wave … is eventually heard as thunder"
- [NWS] National Weather Service, Lightning temperature — https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-temperature — "lightning can heat the air it passes through to 50,000 degrees Fahrenheit (5 times hotter than the surface of the sun)"
- [ED] NASA Earthdata, Lightning — https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/topics/atmosphere/lightning — return stroke "on the order of 30,000 degrees Celsius"; global rate "35 flashes per second during the Northern hemisphere winter to 55 … summer"
- [NTRS] Christian et al. (2003), Global Frequency and Distribution of Lightning as Observed from Space by the OTD, NASA NTRS — https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20020051098 — "an average of 44 +/- 5 lightning flashes … occurring around the globe every second"
- [NWS-F] NWS Flagstaff, Lightning — https://www.weather.gov/fgz/Lightning — "Thunder is the sound produced by rapidly expanding gases along a lightning discharge channel"

## Derived (compute_output.txt)
- 30,000 °C = 54,032 °F → "over 50,000 °F" is true.
- 10,000 °F = 5,538 °C; 5,500 °C = 9,932 °F → "about 10,000 °F" true.
- Ratio 5.0–5.45 depending on scale → "about 5 times".
- "Lightning" = "flashes" incl. cloud-to-cloud; wording: "flashes about 44 times every second" (not "hits the ground").

## Presentation rules
- Temperature bars are drawn proportional in °C (5,500 vs 30,000).
- Earth-flash visual is illustrative, not real data positions.
- Dropped (only one source found): channel width 1–2 cm, return-stroke speed ⅓ light speed.
- Myths avoided: "lightning never strikes twice", 100 flashes/s (outdated).
