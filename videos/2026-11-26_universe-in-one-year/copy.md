# Copy: one-off: the universe's history squeezed into one year (cosmic calendar)

## YouTube title
If the Universe Were ONE Year, Dinosaurs Show Up on CHRISTMAS 🦖🤯

## YouTube description
Squeeze all 13.8 billion years of the universe into one calendar year. The Big Bang is midnight on January 1st, every day is about 38 million years, and every second is over 400 years. By January 8th galaxies already exist (the James Webb telescope has seen one from just 280 million years after the Big Bang). Then nothing for us all through summer: the Sun and Earth only form in early September, and the first life, tiny microbes, shows up in late September. Animals explode across the oceans on December 17th, dinosaurs arrive on Christmas Day, and the asteroid wipes them out on the morning of December 30th. Our species finally shows up at about 11:48 PM on New Year's Eve (about 300,000 years ago). The pyramids? About ten seconds before midnight. And an 80-year life fits in less than a fifth of a second.

Sources: NASA / ESA (Planck, Webb), USGS, Natural History Museum, Max Planck Institute (Jebel Irhoud), Britannica, Carl Sagan's Cosmic Calendar.
