## Scale (compute.py)
Universe age 13.787 billion years (Planck 2018; NASA rounds to 13.8). One 365-day year. 1 day = 37.8 million years ("about thirty-eight million"); 1 second = 437 years ("over four hundred", on screen "≈ 440"). The Big Bang is midnight, Jan 1.

## Claims (each with ≥2 independent sources)
1. **Universe ≈ 13.8 billion years old.** NASA JPL Planck release ("13.8 billion years old"); Wikipedia Cosmic Calendar (13.787 bn, Planck 2018); Space.com.
2. **1 day ≈ 38 million years, 1 second ≈ 440 years.** compute.py (37.8 My, 437 yr); Wikipedia Cosmic Calendar ("437/438 years per cosmic second… 37.8 million years per cosmic day"); Big Think ("each 'day' … around 38 million years"; "each second corresponds to 440 years").
3. **By Jan 8 galaxies already exist; Webb has seen one.** NASA Webb (MoM-z14 seen "only 280 million years after … the big bang"); ESA "Webb gazes further back in time"; EarthSky. compute.py: 280 My after → Jan 8, 09:54.
4. **"All through summer, still no Sun"; the Sun and Earth form in early September.** USGS (solar system 4.53–4.58 bn, Earth 4.54 bn); Sky at Night / Wikipedia Cosmic Calendar (solar system 4.57 bn). compute.py: 4.6 bn → Sep 1; 4.57 → Sep 2; Earth 4.54 → Sep 2. (Summer = Jun–Aug.)
5. **Life: late September, tiny microbes.** 3.5-billion-year-old stromatolites / microbial remains (UNSW 2019; Space.com/Live Science); Wikipedia Cosmic Calendar puts first prokaryote life at 3.8 bn (Sept 21). compute.py: 3.5 bn → Sep 30; 3.8 bn → Sep 22. Both are late September.
6. **Animals explode across the oceans on Dec 17 (Cambrian explosion, ~538.8 Mya).** Britannica (Cambrian Period from 538.8 Mya); Wikipedia "Cambrian explosion" (~538.8 Mya, marine). compute.py → Dec 17, 17:39. (Some animals existed earlier; the narration says they "explode", i.e. the Cambrian explosion.)
7. **Dinosaurs show up on Christmas Day (~230 Mya).** USGS ("oldest dinosaur types … about 230 million years old"); Natural History Museum (~230 Mya); Wikipedia Cosmic Calendar (Dec 25). compute.py: 230–233 My → Dec 25.
8. **The asteroid wipes them out on the morning of Dec 30 (66 Mya).** NSF / Imperial College / Britannica (Chicxulub, 66 Mya); Wikipedia Cosmic Calendar (Dec 30, 6:24); Big Think ("on the morning of December 30th"). compute.py → Dec 30, 06:04.
9. **New Year's Eve 11 PM: "still nobody like us".** compute.py: 11 PM = 1.57 My ago; Homo sapiens 300,000 yr (claim 10). The narration says "nobody like us", i.e. our species. (Earlier human species such as Homo erectus were around then, so the video never says "no humans".)
10. **Our species shows up at about 11:48 PM (≈300,000 years ago).** Max Planck EVA / Hublin et al. 2017, Nature (Jebel Irhoud H. sapiens "dated to 300 thousand years ago"); Natural History Museum; Big Think ("approximately 11:48 PM … about 300,000 years ago"). compute.py → 23:48:33 (280k–315k years → 23:48–23:49; universe age 13.7–13.9 bn → 23:48).
11. **The pyramids: about ten seconds before midnight.** Great Pyramid built c. 2560 BCE (World History Encyclopedia; National Geographic). compute.py: 4,586 years → 10.5 s before midnight (on screen "~10 SECONDS").
12. **If you live to eighty, your whole life fits in less than a fifth of a second, about one blink.** compute.py: 80 yr = 0.183 s < 0.2 s. A blink lasts about 0.1–0.4 s (Wikipedia "Blinking", citing Harvard Database of Useful Biological Numbers; lens.com / ARH Foundation summaries).
13. **Share line: "dinosaurs lived ages ago… on this calendar, it was last week."** Dinosaurs Dec 25–30 vs Dec 31 (claims 7–8) = within the last week of the calendar.
14. **Comment question** is an opinion poll (no claim).
