**Title:** How Fast Are You Moving RIGHT NOW? (Sitting Perfectly Still) 🤯

**YouTube description:**
You're sitting still... but you're not. At the equator, Earth's spin moves the ground at about 1,670 km/h (1,040 mph), almost twice as fast as a jet airliner. Earth also orbits the Sun at about 107,000 km/h (67,000 mph): fast enough to reach the Moon in under 4 hours. And the Sun carries us around the Milky Way at about 828,000 km/h (514,000 mph). One lap takes over 200 million years; all the dinosaurs came and went in less than one lap.

These motions point in different directions, so they don't simply add up. Measured against the afterglow of the Big Bang (the cosmic microwave background), we're moving at about 1.33 million km/h (about 830,000 mph, or 370 km every second). You don't feel any of it because the speed is steady.
