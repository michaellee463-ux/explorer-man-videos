**Title:** What Happens When Sand Hits Earth at 44 Miles PER SECOND? 🌠

**YouTube description:**
Most shooting stars are just grains of sand. This week the Leonid meteor shower returns: dust from comet Tempel-Tuttle (one orbit takes about 33 years) slams into Earth's atmosphere at about 71 km/s (44 miles per second). That's New York to Los Angeles in under a minute, or the Moon in about an hour and a half. A pea-sized Leonid of about half a gram carries roughly twice the energy of a 1.5-ton car at 100 km/h. In 1966, observers reported up to 40 meteors every second. But the grains never reach the ground: around 100 km up, they vaporize in a streak of light.

2026 Leonids: peak around November 17. Look up after midnight, once the Moon has set.
