## YouTube description
Jupiter is 11 Earths wide and has no solid surface. Fall in from deep space and its gravity pulls you to about 60 km/s (130,000+ mph). NASA's Galileo probe hit Jupiter at 106,000 mph in 1995, took more than 200 g, radioed home for about 58 minutes, then went silent at about 23 times Earth's air pressure. Deeper down, pressure reaches millions of times Earth's air and hydrogen becomes a liquid metal. The core is thought to be around 20,000 °C, hotter than the surface of the Sun. You'd never hit the bottom.

Figures: NASA (NSSDC fact sheet, Jupiter facts, Galileo probe), Juno mission (SwRI), ESA, Britannica. Core temperature is a model estimate; estimates vary. Fall speed assumes falling in from far away (Jupiter's escape speed, 59.5 km/s).
