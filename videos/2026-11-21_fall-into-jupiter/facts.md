## Claims (each confirmed by ≥2 independent reputable sources)
1. **Jupiter has no solid surface** — NASA Jupiter facts ("doesn't have a true surface… nowhere to land"), Juno/SwRI interior page ("has no solid surface"), Britannica interior ("No solid surface exists in any of these models"), ESA Juice facts (surface is an arbitrary 1-bar level).
2. **About 11 Earths wide** — NSSDC fact sheet equatorial radius 71,492 km vs 6,378.1 km (ratio 11.21; compute.py), ESA ("11 times that of Earth").
3. **Mostly hydrogen and helium** — NASA facts, NASA Space Place, ESA (~90% H / 10% He).
4. **Falling in from deep space you'd reach ~60 km/s ≈ 130,000 mph** — falling from rest far away, you hit at the escape speed: NSSDC escape velocity 59.5 km/s; recomputed from GM/R = 59.53 km/s (compute.py). 59.5 km/s = 133,098 mph. Said as "about sixty kilometers per second… over a hundred and thirty thousand miles per hour". Ignores Jupiter's spin and the air (stated as "about").
5. **Fast enough to circle Earth in about 11 minutes** — 40,075 km ÷ 59.5 km/s = 11.2 min (compute.py).
6. **Gravity more than twice Earth's** — NSSDC 2.36× (equator) to 2.75× (pole), mean 2.64×; ESA "2.5 times".
7. **Storms bigger than Earth** — NASA facts ("a giant storm bigger than Earth"), ESA (Great Red Spot "still larger than Earth").
8. **NASA's Galileo probe hit Jupiter at 106,000 mph on Dec 7, 1995** — NASA Galileo probe page ("106,000 mph (170,590 km/h)"), NASA "65 amazing Galileo facts" ("106,000 miles per hour"), Galileo Probe quick-look science summary (170,700 km/h / 106,000 mph). Computed 47.4 km/s.
9. **It was the first probe to enter a giant planet's atmosphere** — NSSDC entry timeline, NASA FAQ ("first atmospheric probe to examine a gas giant").
10. **It felt more than 200 g** — NASA probe page (228 g in-depth; 350 g in trivia), quick-look summary (230 g), NASA 65 facts ("250 times heavier"). All > 200, so "more than two hundred times Earth's gravity".
11. **Entry heating hotter than the Sun's surface** — quick-look summary ("temperatures twice as hot as the Sun's surface" during the first two minutes); NASA probe page (16,000 °C about a minute after entry) vs Sun's surface 5,500 °C (NASA Sun facts, UCAR).
12. **It radioed for about 58 minutes, then went silent at ~23 times Earth's air pressure** — NASA probe page (data for 58 minutes; failure "due to the enormous pressure (22.7 atmospheres)"), quick-look summary (57.6 minutes; 23 bars).
13. **Deeper, pressure reaches millions of times Earth's air pressure and hydrogen becomes a liquid metal** — Juno/SwRI (~2 million bars → "liquid metallic hydrogen"), NASA facts (pressure makes hydrogen "electrically conducting like metal"), Britannica (molecular→metallic hydrogen transition; centre 50–100 million atm). The layer is tens of thousands of km thick under any source's transition depth (28,000–45,000 km; compute.py) → said as "an ocean of liquid metal, thousands of kilometers deep" (true under all models).
14. **Core around 20,000 °C or more — hotter than the Sun's surface** — Juno/SwRI (~20,000 °C; 16,000–30,000+ °C), NASA Space Place (~24,000 °C), Britannica (~24,700 °C) vs Sun surface 5,500 °C (NASA, UCAR). Ratio 2.9–5.5× (compute.py) → said only as "hotter than the surface of the Sun".
15. **Anything falling in is crushed, melted and vaporized long before the centre** — NASA facts ("crush, melt, and vaporize spacecraft trying to fly into the planet"), Juno/SwRI (pressure and heat "would destroy the spacecraft"), NSSDC timeline (probe "completely vaporized").

Dropped: "top clouds are ammonia ice" (contested — ESO 2025 analysis suggests the upper clouds are mostly ammonium hydrosulfide); Great Red Spot width (NASA "twice as wide" is outdated; it has shrunk to ~1.3 Earths); wind speeds (sources disagree: 539 vs 680 km/h); probe depth in km (sources differ by reference level).
